Political Authority as a Central Subject of Contemporary Political Science

Seven sessions of 60–90 minutes each, without an assumed deadline. Each session: 5 minutes of recall, 35–55 minutes of reading or writing, and 20–30 minutes of application and summary.

Day Focus Task Deliverable

Reading order

  • [P1]: Section 13.1, especially Authority and Legitimacy.
  • [P2]: Articles 1, 2, 6, 70, and 89: examples of institutional allocation, not evidence of public acceptance.

Exercise

An official has enforcement resources but exceeds the assigned powers. What is the legal question, and what evidence would be needed to investigate public trust?

Answer guidance: The legal question concerns the basis and limits of competence. Trust is an empirical issue requiring independent evidence; the legal text alone cannot establish it.

Active recall

  • Explain the research question without opening the files.
  • What does each source support, and what does it not establish?
  • Explain the example and justify the outcome.
  • What still needs checking before submission?

When studying all five courses together, complete session 1 for each, then session 2, and so on. The whole programme involves about 35 sessions; it is not a requirement to complete five courses in seven days.

Report outline

Political Authority as a Central Subject of Contemporary Political Science

Research question

How does political authority differ from mere coercive capacity, and how are legal authorization and legitimacy related?

Structure

  1. Introduction: why studying authority requires more than describing force.
  2. Part I: power, authorization, and legitimacy.
  3. Part II: Moroccan institutional illustrations and the limits of inference from texts.
  4. Conclusion: distinguish legal, empirical, and normative questions.

Scope and length

The supplied report is a complete concise treatment, not a 1,500-word research paper. The photos do not specify a numerical word count. If your instructor requires 1,200–1,600 words, expand the evidence discussion and application, adding verified references rather than repeating definitions. Suggested allocation: introduction 15%, analysis 70%, conclusion 15%.

Paragraph method

Claim → cited evidence → your analysis → specific example → implication for the question.
