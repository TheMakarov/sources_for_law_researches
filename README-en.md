One topic has been selected for each course in your photos, including the original law topic. ## How to use it

1. Open index.html and choose a course and language.
2. Start with the study plan and designated readings.
3. Read the saved sources, then use the report to understand and develop your own writing.
4. Record evidence in the notes and compare bilingual terminology.
5. Match the submission copy to your actual instructor requirements.

## Folder structure

Each course contains ar/, en/, sources/ for original-language material, and assignment/ for your photos. Markdown files are editable; HTML pages are ready to read or print in a browser. No installation or local server is required.

## Scope

- Reports are complete concise treatments, not long papers with an invented mandatory word count.
- Arabic and English versions match in argument and references; they are not official translations of legislation or fiqh.
- Bilingual study materials do not mean that every original source exists in both languages. The register identifies source formats and editions.
- Constitutional examples use Morocco’s 2011 Constitution. The obligations code copy is consolidated through 22 August 2024; check later amendments affecting the examples before submission.
- Foreign theoretical and comparative materials do not establish empirical findings about Morocco.
- The Islamic-law photo mentions a concise sourced report, independent work, and submission before autumn exams, but gives no calendar date. No deadline has been invented. The constitutional-law photo asks for one focused question; question 6 was selected.
- Allow seven sessions per course, about 35 sessions total, at 60–90 minutes each.
- Text snapshots are technical extractions of publisher webpages and may include navigation elements; they are not original PDFs.
